CONCLUSION

Before cutting stations, check whether the hills are short of bikes

1. Many low-use stations are short of bikes, not of demand: 128 of 427 merge candidates already run empty, half of those at 40 m and above.

2. Morning shortage rises with elevation and climb. Subway access, rain and housing do not explain it.

3. Even at the busiest hour, 89% of bikes are parked. The issue is where bikes are, not how many.

Proposal: when choosing stations to merge, check morning shortage as well as ride counts, and move bikes up the hills before the morning peak.

Back to our question: are the least-used stations short of demand, or short of bikes? For many of them, it is bikes. Of the 427 merge candidates, 128 already run empty in the morning, and at 40 metres and above it is half. That shortage rises with elevation and climb, and subway access, rain and housing do not explain it. And the fleet itself is large enough: even at 6 p.m., the busiest hour, 89% of bikes are standing at stations. So our proposal: when choosing stations to merge, do not look at ride counts alone. Look at morning shortage too, and for hill-top stations, try rebalancing bikes before the morning peak before you cut them.
